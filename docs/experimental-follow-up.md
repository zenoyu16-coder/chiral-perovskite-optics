# Experimental considerations

The remaining physical question concerns polarization retention after excitation: how do handedness, structural order and optical measurement geometry influence the time evolution of circularly polarized emission?

A useful comparison would require a common material platform with R, S and an achiral reference, with the same nominal layer composition and matched synthesis. For the superlattice system, a PEA reference must be characterized for differences in growth and optical confinement; its chemical and structural differences from R/S-MBA are relevant to interpreting any threshold change.

Each independently prepared batch should include structural and optical checks (XRD, absorption, PL and film/domain orientation). Polarization-resolved measurements should record analyzer calibration, front/back acquisition or sample rotation, linear optical anisotropy, and separate left- and right-circular emission channels.

A time-resolved experiment should include the system's instrument response and the same pump fluences for all specimens, with multiple independent preparations and repeated acquisitions. The primary observables are the total time-resolved PL intensity and `[I_LCP(t)-I_RCP(t)]/[I_LCP(t)+I_RCP(t)]`, with the signal-to-noise window reported.

A minimal feasibility study could begin with repeatable steady-state CPL/PL from a small R/S batch set and proceed to time-resolved polarization only after demonstrating reproducible optical handedness under changes in sample orientation. A change in polarization contrast is not by itself a spin-relaxation measurement; any spin-specific interpretation needs independent spin-sensitive evidence.

There is no experimental dataset or laboratory access associated with this proposal in the repository. No fabrication or new measurement has been conducted as part of this project.
